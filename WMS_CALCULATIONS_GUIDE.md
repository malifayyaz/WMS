# WMS Reports & Calculations Guide

This document explains exactly how numbers are calculated in the Reports section of the Wire Manufacturing Management System (WMS). It is written for business owners and management to understand the origin of every figure shown on the screen. It also includes links to the backend code where these calculations are performed.

---

## 1. PROFIT & LOSS (P&L) REPORT
**Source Code:** [profitReportService.js](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/utils/profitReportService.js) | [reportController.js](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/controllers/reportController.js)

The Profit & Loss report measures how much money the business actually earned from operations during a specific period. It uses the **accrual method**, meaning it counts sales and purchases when the goods are physically delivered, regardless of when the customer actually pays for them.

### Revenue (Total Revenue)
**Explanation:** The total income generated from selling your own wire, plus the income earned from processing other people's wire (labour charges), plus any money made selling excess wire leftover from job-work, minus the value of any wire that customers returned to you.

**Formula:**
`Total Revenue = (Total Kg of Wire Sold × Average Sale Rate per Kg) + Job-Work Labour Income + Job-Work Excess Wire Sales − (Total Kg of Wire Returned × Average Sale Rate per Kg of returns)`

**Example:**
1. You sell 1,000 kg of wire at Rs. 300 per kg. (Value: 300,000).
2. You earn Rs. 50,000 from job-work labour for a client.
3. A customer returns 100 kg of wire originally sold at Rs. 300 per kg. (Value: 30,000).
*Total Revenue = 300,000 + 50,000 − 30,000 = **Rs. 320,000**.*

**Conditions:** This does *not* include money collected from old debts or past sales. It only counts sales made within the selected dates.

### Cost of Wire Sold & Weighted Average Cost
**Explanation:** This represents the cost of the raw material (coil) used to manufacture the wire you just sold. Because you buy coil at different prices at different times, the system blends these costs together using a "Weighted Average" to find the true cost of your current inventory.

**Formula:**
`Weighted Average Purchase Rate = (Total Value of Opening Coil Stock + Total Value of Newly Purchased Coil) ÷ (Total Kg of Opening Coil Stock + Total Kg of Newly Purchased Coil)`
*then...*
`Cost of Wire Sold = Total Kg of Wire Sold × Weighted Average Purchase Rate`

**Example:**
1. You start the month with 500 kg of coil bought at Rs. 200/kg (Total Value: 100,000). 
2. You buy another 500 kg at Rs. 220/kg (Total Value: 110,000). 
3. You now have 1,000 kg total worth 210,000. Your new Weighted Average Rate is Rs. 210/kg (210,000 ÷ 1,000).
4. If you sell 400 kg of wire, the Cost of Wire Sold is 400 kg × 210 = **Rs. 84,000**.

### Impact of Waste Percentage
**Explanation:** During manufacturing, a certain amount of raw material is lost as scale, dust, or scrap. The system automatically assumes a set percentage of the material was wasted during production, which increases your overall cost of goods sold.

**Formula:**
`Waste Amount = Cost of Wire Sold × (Waste Percentage ÷ 100)`
*then...*
`Final Cost of Wire Sold = Cost of Wire Sold + Waste Amount`

**Example:**
1. Your initial Cost of Wire Sold is Rs. 100,000.
2. The factory waste setting is 2.5%. 
3. Waste Amount = 100,000 × 0.025 = 2,500.
4. Final Cost of Wire Sold = 100,000 + 2,500 = **Rs. 102,500**.

### Gross Profit
**Explanation:** The raw profit made purely from manufacturing and selling goods, before paying for factory overheads, bills, or personal withdrawals.

**Formula:**
`Gross Profit = Total Revenue − Final Cost of Wire Sold`

**Example:**
1. Total Revenue is Rs. 320,000.
2. Final Cost of Wire Sold is Rs. 102,500.
*Gross Profit = 320,000 − 102,500 = **Rs. 217,500**.*

### Factory Expenses
**Explanation:** The everyday operational costs of running the factory, such as electricity, worker salaries, machinery maintenance, and consumable materials used in processing.

**Formula:**
`Factory Expenses = (Sum of all recorded Expenses NOT marked as "Self Expense") + (Sum of all Process Materials purchased, like acid or chemicals)`

**Example:**
1. You pay Rs. 20,000 for electricity and Rs. 30,000 for salaries.
2. You buy Rs. 5,000 worth of acid (Process Material).
*Factory Expenses = 20,000 + 30,000 + 5,000 = **Rs. 55,000**.*

### Operating Profit
**Explanation:** The true profit generated by the factory as a business, after paying all manufacturing and operational costs, but before owner withdrawals.

**Formula:**
`Operating Profit = Gross Profit − Factory Expenses`

**Example:**
1. Gross Profit is Rs. 215,000.
2. Factory Expenses are Rs. 55,000.
*Operating Profit = 215,000 − 55,000 = **Rs. 160,000**.*

### Final Net Profit
**Explanation:** The final amount left over for the business after deducting the owners' personal withdrawals (Self Expenses) from the Operating Profit.

**Formula:**
`Final Net Profit = Operating Profit − Self Expenses`

**Example:**
1. Operating Profit is Rs. 160,000.
2. The owners withdrew Rs. 40,000 for personal household use (Self Expense).
*Final Net Profit = 160,000 − 40,000 = **Rs. 120,000**.*

## 2. FINANCIAL REPORT
**Source Code:** [reportController.js (getFinancialReport)](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/controllers/reportController.js#L31-L100)

The Financial Report shows physical money moving in and out of your hands and bank accounts. Unlike the P&L, this report only cares about *when* the cash was received or paid, not when the actual sale or purchase took place (Cash Basis).

### Money In vs Money Out
**Explanation:** A summary of all physical cash and bank deposits received, versus all cash payments, expenses, and bank transfers sent out.

**Formula:**
`Total Money In = All Cash Received + All Bank Transfers Received`
`Total Money Out = All Cash Paid to Suppliers + All Factory Expenses + All Self Expenses + All Bank Transfers Paid`

**Example:**
1. You collect Rs. 50,000 from an old customer debt, and get a Rs. 10,000 bank transfer. Total Money In = **Rs. 60,000**.
2. You pay Rs. 5,000 for lunch (Self Expense) and Rs. 15,000 to a supplier. Total Money Out = **Rs. 20,000**.

## 3. INVENTORY REPORT
**Source Code:** [reportController.js (getInventoryReport)](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/controllers/reportController.js#L119-L207)

This report shows exactly what physical materials are currently sitting in the factory or at the annealer.

### Current Coil Stock (Raw Stock)
**Explanation:** The total weight and value of raw coil you currently own in the warehouse.

**Formula:**
`Total Coil Kg = Total Kg of purchased coil − Total Kg returned to suppliers − Total Kg drawn into wire`

**Conditions:** This does *not* include coil that has been sent to the annealer (Bhatti) or coil that is currently being processed on machines. Those are tracked in separate "Annealing Pending" or "Processing Stock" pools.

### Low Stock Alerts
**Explanation:** An automatic warning triggered to let you know you are running out of raw material for a specific type of coil.

**Formula:**
`If (Total Coil Kg for a specific category < 1,000 Kg) -> Trigger Alert`

### Ready / Finished Wire Stock
**Explanation:** Wire that has been manufactured and is sitting in bundles ready to be sold to customers.

**Formula:**
`Ready Wire Kg = Total Kg of wire produced − Total Kg of wire sold + Total Kg of wire returned by customers`

## 4. CUSTOMER REPORT (LEDGERS)
**Source Code:** [ledgerService.js](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/utils/ledgerService.js) | [customerController.js](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/controllers/customerController.js)

This tracks how much a customer owes you, or how much advance money they have deposited with you.

### Purchased / Paid / Due (Balance)
**Explanation:** The running tally of a customer's account balance over time. 

**Formula:**
`Customer Due (Balance) = Opening Balance + Total Value of Purchases − Total Payments Received`

**Example:**
1. A customer started the year owing Rs. 10,000 (Opening Balance).
2. They bought Rs. 40,000 worth of wire.
3. They paid you Rs. 20,000.
*Due = 10,000 + 40,000 − 20,000 = **Rs. 30,000**.*

**Conditions Based on Customer Type:**
* **Ledger (Regular) Customer:** The "Total Value of Purchases" adds the full cost of the raw wire sold to them.
* **Processing Customer:** The "Total Value of Purchases" only adds the *Labour* charges for processing their coil, plus the value of any *Excess Wire* you sold them from the yield. It does not charge them for the raw wire since they provided it.
* **Daily Customer:** Always starts with an Opening Balance of 0, because they are expected to pay in cash immediately.

## 5. DAILY BOOK REPORT
**Source Code:** [dailyBookReportService.js](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/utils/dailyBookReportService.js) | [cashBookService.js](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/utils/cashBookService.js) | [bankBalanceService.js](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/utils/bankBalanceService.js)

This is the daily tally used by the manager to match the physical cash drawer and verify the day's activity.

### Daily Cash Opening / In / Out / Closing
**Explanation:** The exact count of physical cash in the drawer.

**Formula:**
`Opening Cash = Yesterday's Closing Cash (or a manual correction entered by the manager)`
`Cash In = All Money received in physical cash today`
`Cash Out = All Money paid in physical cash today + Today's Cash Factory Expenses + Today's Cash Self Expenses`
`Closing Cash = Opening Cash + Cash In − Cash Out`

**Conditions:** Bank transfers and company cheques are strictly excluded from these Cash figures to prevent double-counting, as they do not affect the physical cash drawer.

### Bank Book Opening / Balance
**Explanation:** The running balance of the company's specific bank accounts (e.g., MBL, UBL).

**Formula:**
`Closing Bank Balance = Opening Bank Balance + Bank Transfers Received − Bank Transfers Sent − Company Cheques Issued`

## 6. BALANCE SHEET
**Source Code:** [balanceSheetController.js](file:///d:/Ali%20Fayaz%20Projects/wms/backend/controllers/balanceSheetController.js)

The Balance Sheet provides a mathematical snapshot of the total wealth and health of the business on a specific past or present date. 

### Assets (What the business owns or is owed)
**Explanation:** The total value of everything the business holds, including cash, stock, and money owed by outsiders.

**Formula:**
`Total Assets = Cash in Drawer + Bank Balances + Value of Raw Coil Stock + Value of Ready Wire Stock + Value of Wire at Annealer + Value of Wire in Processing + Receivables`
*(Receivables = Money owed by customers + Money owed by processing clients + Advances given to suppliers + Advances given to annealers + Personal loans given)*

**Conditions:** Inventory values here are reconstructed based on the Weighted Average Rate of the raw material on the exact date you select.

### Liabilities (What the business owes)
**Explanation:** The total amount of debts the business owes to outsiders.

**Formula:**
`Total Liabilities = Supplier Payables + Annealing Payables + Customer Advances + Personal Payables`
*(Supplier Payables = Unpaid bills for coil and process materials. Annealing Payables = Unpaid bills from the Bhatti. Customer Advances = Money customers deposited before taking goods).*

### Net Worth (Equity)
**Explanation:** The true bottom-line value of the business to the owners. If you sold all your stock, collected all debts, and paid off everyone you owe, this is the cash you would have left.

**Formula:**
`Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities`

**Example:**
1. You have Rs. 500,000 in Total Assets (cash, wire, receivables).
2. You owe Rs. 100,000 in Total Liabilities (to suppliers and annealers).
*Net Worth = 500,000 − 100,000 = **Rs. 400,000**.*

**Condition:** If you look at the Balance Sheet for a date in the past, the system automatically simulates reversing every single sale, purchase, and payment that happened since then to reconstruct exactly what the balances were on that specific day.

## Glossary of Terms

* **Accrual:** A method of accounting that counts income and expenses when the actual sale or purchase happens, regardless of when the cash actually changes hands.
* **Gross Profit:** The profit made directly from manufacturing and selling goods, before paying factory overheads or owner withdrawals.
* **Weighted Average Cost:** A blended cost of your inventory, created by mathematically averaging the cheap and expensive batches of coil you bought over time.
* **Receivables:** Money that other people (usually customers) owe to your business.
* **Payables:** Money that your business owes to other people (usually suppliers or annealers).
* **Net Worth:** The absolute bottom-line value of the business; what would be left if you sold all assets and paid off all debts today.
